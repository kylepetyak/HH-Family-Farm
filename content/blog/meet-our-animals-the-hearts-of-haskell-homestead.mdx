They say a homestead is only as good as its animals, and if that's true, we're doing pretty well. Our animals aren't just livestock—they're family members with names, personalities, and very strong opinions about treat time. Let me introduce you to the crew.

## The Horses: Our Gentle Giants

### Luna
Luna is our 12-year-old Quarter Horse mare, and she is 100% the boss of this homestead. She came to us three years ago from a rescue situation, and while it took time to earn her trust, she's now the most loyal companion you could ask for.

**Personality:** Protective, dignified, secretly loves carrots but acts like she's doing you a favor by accepting them

**Fun fact:** Luna will not let you catch her in the pasture. But if you sit down and wait, she'll come investigate within 10 minutes. Every time.

### Biscuit
Biscuit is our 8-year-old Paint gelding, and he is the complete opposite of Luna. Where she is reserved and regal, Biscuit is basically a giant golden retriever in horse form.

**Personality:** Goofy, affectionate, will absolutely knock you over trying to get into your pockets for treats

**Fun fact:** He's terrified of plastic bags but will walk right up to the pigs without a second thought

## The Pigs: Our Comedians

Oh, the pigs. If you've never spent time with pigs, you might not know that they're basically dogs with snouts. Smart, social, and absolutely hilarious.

### Maple & Rosie
These two Kunekune sisters came to us as piglets, and they've grown into the most entertaining members of our homestead.

**Personality:** Maple is the adventurous one, always investigating new corners of the pasture. Rosie is the snuggler who will fall asleep on your lap if you let her (yes, even at 150 pounds).

**Fun fact:** They know their names and come running when called. They also know the sound of the treat container opening and will scream (yes, scream) until they get their snacks.

### Why Kunekunes?
We chose Kunekune pigs specifically because they're a heritage breed known for being gentle, relatively small (for pigs), and great with kids. They're primarily grazers, which works perfectly for our 1.5 acres. Plus, those fuzzy faces? Come on.

## The Chickens: Our Original Homestead Animals

Our chicken flock is where our homesteading journey began, and they hold a special place in our hearts.

### The Current Flock
We have 8 hens right now, a mix of breeds chosen for both egg production and personality:

- **3 Rhode Island Reds** (reliable layers, a bit bossy)
- **2 Buff Orpingtons** (the sweetest, most docile girls)
- **2 Easter Eggers** (for those beautiful blue-green eggs)
- **1 Silkie** (purely for entertainment—she's ridiculous and we love her)

### Henrietta the Escape Artist
Every flock has that ONE chicken, and for us, it's Henrietta, our most determined Rhode Island Red. No matter how we reinforce the fence, she finds a way out. At this point, we've just accepted that Henrietta lives by her own rules. She always comes home to roost, so we've learned to let her do her thing.

## Why Animals Matter to Our Homestead

People sometimes ask why we have animals beyond just the practical reasons. Yes, the chickens give us eggs. Yes, the horses provide transportation around the property. But the real reason?

**Connection.**

In a world of screens and schedules, our animals ground us. They require us to be present, to pay attention, to slow down. When my daughter is having a hard day, she goes and sits with the pigs. When my son is bursting with energy, he helps with horse chores. When I need to decompress after work, morning feed time is my meditation.

Our animals teach our kids about responsibility, life cycles, compassion, and hard work. They teach us adults about patience, presence, and the simple joy of an animal who's happy to see you.

## Thinking About Adding Animals to Your Homestead?

Here's my advice: start with chickens. They're forgiving of beginner mistakes, relatively easy to care for, and give you something back (eggs!) almost immediately. Once you've got the chicken thing down, then think about expanding.

And remember: every animal you add to your homestead is a commitment. They depend on you 365 days a year, in good weather and bad, when you're feeling motivated and when you're not. Make sure you're ready for that before bringing any creature home.
