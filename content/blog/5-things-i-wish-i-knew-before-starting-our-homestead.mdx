When we first decided to turn our 1.5 acres in Fallbrook into a homestead, I had Pinterest boards full of dreamy farm aesthetics and a head full of romantic notions about country living. Don't get me wrong—homesteading has been one of the most rewarding decisions we've ever made—but there are definitely some things I wish someone had told me before we jumped in.

## 1. Start Small (Seriously, I Mean It)

I know, I know. You've seen the Instagram accounts with the sprawling gardens, multiple animal species, and picture-perfect barns. Trust me, they didn't build that overnight.

When we first started, I wanted it all: chickens, goats, a huge vegetable garden, maybe even a cow. What I got was overwhelming exhaustion and half-finished projects everywhere.

**What I wish I'd done:** Start with one thing. Master it. Then add another. We started with just three chickens, and honestly, that was the perfect introduction to animal husbandry.

## 2. The Real Cost Isn't What You Think

Sure, I budgeted for fencing, coops, and feed. What I didn't budget for? The vet bills, the predator-proofing upgrades after we lost a hen, the water system that needed replacing, and the endless "small" purchases that add up fast.

**My advice:** Whatever budget you have in mind, add 50%. Then add a little more for the "oops, I didn't think of that" moments. Because there will be many.

## 3. Your Timeline Will Be Wrong

I thought we'd have a fully functioning homestead within a year. Three years later, we're still building, learning, and adjusting. And you know what? That's okay.

Homesteading is a marathon, not a sprint. The soil needs time to improve. Animals need time to adjust. YOU need time to learn what works for your specific land, climate, and lifestyle.

## 4. Community Is Everything

I used to think homesteading was about self-sufficiency—doing everything ourselves. But the truth is, our homestead thrives because of our community.

- The neighbor who taught us about local soil conditions
- The Facebook group that helped diagnose our chicken's weird behavior
- The local feed store owner who always has advice (and occasionally discounts)

Don't try to do this alone. Find your people, online and off.

## 5. It's Okay to Fail

Our first garden was a disaster. Seriously. I planted everything at the wrong time, overwatered some plants, underwatered others, and attracted every pest in San Diego County.

But here's what I learned: every failure is just research for next season. Keep notes, take photos, and be gentle with yourself. Even experienced homesteaders have off years.

---

## The Bottom Line

Starting a homestead is one of the most challenging and rewarding things you can do. It will test your patience, your budget, and occasionally your sanity. But watching your kids collect eggs from chickens you raised, eating vegetables from soil you nurtured, and creating a life connected to the land—that's something no amount of preparation can fully capture.

If you're thinking about starting your homesteading journey, my biggest piece of advice is this: **just start**. Start small, start imperfect, but start. You'll figure the rest out along the way.
