One of the questions I get asked most often is: "How do you do it all?" The honest answer? I don't. Not perfectly, anyway. But we've found a rhythm that works for our family, and I wanted to share what a typical day looks like at Haskell Homestead.

## The Early Morning: 5:30 AM

Yes, I know. But here's the thing about homestead life—the animals don't care if you want to sleep in.

My alarm goes off at 5:30, and I'll be honest, I hit snooze at least once. By 5:45, I'm pulling on my farm boots (which live by the back door because tracking barn muck through the house is not an option).

**Morning chores (about 45 minutes):**
- Let the chickens out and refresh their water
- Check on the pigs and give them breakfast
- Quick visual check on the horses
- Water the garden if it didn't get done the night before
- Collect any early eggs

The best part of morning chores? The quiet. It's just me, the animals, and the most beautiful Fallbrook sunrises you can imagine.

## The Chaos Begins: 6:30 AM

This is when the kids wake up, and the real juggling act starts.

While my husband (when he's not on shift at the fire station) handles breakfast, I'm usually throwing together lunches and trying to remember if anyone has a permission slip due. Mornings are loud, messy, and almost always involve someone crying about socks. (Why is it always socks?)

## Work Mode: 8:00 AM - 3:00 PM

I work from home, which is both a blessing and a challenge when you're a homesteader. The blessing: I can throw in laundry, start dinner in the slow cooker, and peek at the animals during breaks. The challenge: the temptation to "just quickly" check on something and lose an hour to barn projects.

My trick? Strict boundaries. Work hours are work hours. The homestead will survive until 3 PM.

## After School Chaos: 3:00 PM - 5:00 PM

This is when the kids get involved in homestead life, and it's honestly my favorite part of the day.

**Our afternoon routine:**
- Kids change into "barn clothes" (aka clothes I don't mind getting destroyed)
- Everyone has age-appropriate chores
- The 8-year-old feeds and waters the chickens
- The 5-year-old "helps" collect eggs (and by helps, I mean carries the basket very seriously)
- Everyone gets animal time—because sometimes you just need to hug a pig

## The Evening Hustle: 5:00 PM - 8:00 PM

Dinner, homework, baths, bedtime stories. You know the drill. This part of our day looks pretty much like any other family's, except our dinner conversations often include topics like "Why do chickens do that thing with their neck?" and "Can we PLEASE get a goat?"

(The answer to the goat question is "we're thinking about it," which is basically a yes.)

## Night Chores: 8:00 PM

Once the kids are in bed, it's time for the final animal check:
- Lock up the chickens (predators are very real in our area)
- Make sure everyone has water for the night
- Quick hay check for the horses
- One more pig belly rub (for them, but honestly, for me too)

## When My Husband Is On Shift

Real talk: when my husband is working a 48-hour shift at the fire station, everything I just described gets harder. Solo parenting PLUS homestead chores is no joke.

On those days, I rely heavily on:
- Meal prep done ahead of time
- Simpler chore routines (survival mode is real)
- Letting go of perfectionism
- Early bedtimes for everyone, including me

## The Truth About Balance

Here's what I want you to know: there is no perfect balance. Some days, the house is a mess because I spent extra time in the garden. Some days, the animals get the bare minimum because work was demanding. Some days, I hide in the bathroom for five minutes of peace.

But most days? Most days we figure it out. The kids are learning responsibility, connection to nature, and where their food comes from. My husband and I are building something meaningful together. And our 1.5 acres is slowly becoming the homestead of our dreams.

Is it a lot? Absolutely. Would I trade it? Not for anything.
